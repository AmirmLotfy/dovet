## Judge testing experience

The `/judges` page starts with the exact three-step fixture flow and a short explanation of local versus hosted adapters. Provide functioning owner-funded access, not just a cloud form that requires a judge's credit card or a paid provider key. Public anti-abuse protections must not remove the judging path. Include any dedicated judge credentials privately in the submission's testing instructions.

A recording is supporting evidence, not a substitute for the promised working test build. Preserve an installable tagged release as an additional route. Explain operating-system assumptions and what a judge can test without connecting personal source code.

## Publication order

Freeze a verified build, publish source/assets, check public website, publish and verify the video, then finalize Devpost and save the receipt. Optional build posts come after the required path is safe, or are drafted while unrelated uploads process. Keep every submitted URL and its verified timestamp in `links.json`.

## Owner confirmation sheet

Record explicit owner review of legal eligibility, ownership and third-party licenses, public source release, synthetic voice rights, domain purchase if applicable, allowed cloud spend and final submission. Do not put personal legal identifiers in a public repository. Codex may fill draft fields, but it must not invent an AWS Builder ID, sign an attestation on someone's behalf without authorization or bypass MFA.

## Freeze strategy

Reserve at least eight hours for recording, export, upload processing and submission troubleshooting. Freeze the source/tag used in the video. Document any permitted later amendment instead of replacing the judged build silently. Keep working test access available through the published judging end date and recheck organizer updates.

## What not to claim

Do not claim first-ever continuity infrastructure, seamless support for every coding agent, guaranteed quota visibility, subscription-limit bypass, zero data leaving the computer, guaranteed security, a market-validated price, measured token savings without experiments or a likelihood of winning. The demonstrated product is enough.
